Opening

Someone has to read it, and then do something about it.

That is the whole job. Everything else in marketing is downstream of whether a stranger read the line and moved.

I have spent thirteen years writing in four industries that could not be less alike. Cinema, where you have a poster and a weekend. Aviation, where the person reading is either booking a holiday or chartering a jet and you cannot talk to both the same way. Global education, where the buyer is a nineteen-year-old and the payer is their mother.

And for the last six years, online gaming, where a line has to be persuasive, on brand and legally defensible in the same breath, in a market whose rules changed underneath everyone halfway through.

What follows is a selection rather than an archive. I have cut everything that was there to fill a page. What is left is work I would defend in a room.

I have also included the thinking, not just the output. A headline on its own never explains why it worked, and the ability to explain why is the difference between a writer you brief and a writer you hand a problem to.

I own voice, campaigns and CRM end to end, in three languages, in industries where a line has to be persuasive and legally defensible at the same time.

How I work

Four things I believe, after thirteen years of being wrong.

01

The constraint is usually the idea.

A legal restriction, a character limit, a market that will not accept the joke that worked next door. Writers treat these as damage. In practice the constraint is the only thing standing between you and the version everybody else already wrote.

02

Voice is a system, not a mood.

“Fun but premium” is not a tone of voice, it is a feeling someone had in a meeting. A voice that survives contact with twelve writers and three markets needs rules you can break on purpose. I have built these, and I have had to enforce them.

03

Ship it, read it, rewrite it.

Performance data settles arguments that would otherwise be settled by whoever is most senior in the room. I would rather put two honest versions live than spend a week defending one in a deck.

04

Translation is not localisation.

A campaign that works in English and is then rendered accurately into Portuguese is a campaign that now works in English and lands flat in Brazil. I write in three languages, which mostly means I know when a line cannot survive the trip.

Case 01 · ARRiSE · iGaming
Content Coordinator, LATAM · Oct 2024 — present
01

Eight launches a month, three languages, one voice.

A global gaming supplier ships new product faster than most brands ship campaigns. Every launch needs an angle, and it cannot be last month’s angle.

The situation

LATAM is not one market. Brazil rewrote its licensing rules while everyone was mid-flight, and what you are allowed to promise a player in São Paulo is not what you can promise in Santiago or Mexico City. The same launch has to work in Portuguese, Spanish and English, past three sets of expectations and one regulator who is still deciding what it thinks.

What I own

Brand voice and campaign copy across the region. Product launch material. Market adaptation rather than translation. The editorial standards the region writes to. And the seat between marketing, product, legal and the local commercial teams, which is where most of the actual work happens.

The thinking

At that cadence the real risk is not a weak line. It is a format that quietly hardens. The eighth launch of the month starts to resemble the first, and nobody notices from the inside because everybody is busy shipping.

So I stopped reviewing case by case and wrote the standard down instead: what each launch format has to achieve, what it is not allowed to reuse, and where compliance sits in the flow rather than bolted onto the end of it.

Reviewing work is a bottleneck. Setting the standard the work gets written to is not.

Case 02 · GingaBet · Sports betting
Copywriter · Jul 2024 — Aug 2025 · freelance, concurrent
02

A tone of voice that could survive contact with the data.

I inherited a tone-of-voice document that described a feeling. Feelings do not settle arguments at four in the afternoon with a campaign going out at six.

The situation

A Brazilian betting brand writing across social, CRM, paid and on-site, with several people producing copy and no shared answer to what the brand sounds like when it loses.

What I did

Campaign concepts, video scripts and promotional copy across every channel. Then rebuilt the tone-of-voice guide around decisions rather than adjectives, and iterated the messaging off performance data instead of whoever felt strongest in the meeting.

The thinking

A usable voice guide answers questions a writer actually has at speed. How long is a sentence. Do we use the second person. What do we say the morning after a bad result.

Guidelines are not a manifesto. They are a tool for the person writing at five to six.

Case 04 · Aviatrix · Localisation
Copywriter & Localisation Specialist · Feb 2024 — Dec 2024 · freelance
04

Every word is correct. The line is dead.

This is what accurate translation does to marketing copy.

What I did

Rebuilt rather than translated. The cultural reference changes because the original one means nothing here. The regulatory wording changes because it has to. The player-facing tone changes because Brazilian players are not addressed the way British ones are.

The thinking

A translator is measured on fidelity to the source. A copywriter is measured on what the reader does next.

Freelance · Tiffany & Co. · Luxury retail
Copywriter, freelance · text only — presentation design below is my own
05

Luxury doesn’t need more adjectives.

It needs fewer of them, placed correctly. A freelance brief where the entire job was restraint.

The brief

Product and campaign copy for Tiffany & Co., delivered as text only, no layout brief attached. The presentation here, including this design, is my own treatment for portfolio purposes.

The thinking

Luxury copy fails in one of two directions: it lists superlatives until they cancel each other out, or it whispers so hard it says nothing. The brief was to write like the object was already in the room and didn’t need selling, only naming well.

Product copy · Jewelry collection
A line, not a chain.

Worn close, closed with a clasp you will stop noticing by the second wear. The kind of piece that gets asked about once, in passing, by someone who already knows the answer.

Made to be inherited, eventually. Worn daily, until then.

Case 06 · EF Education First · Global education
Senior Copywriter & Content Specialist · Jan 2019 — Jan 2023 · concurrent
06

The reader is nineteen. The person paying is not.

Study-abroad copy has to excite a teenager and reassure a parent, usually in the same paragraph.

The thinking

The category default is to sell the destination: sunsets, skylines, friends you have not met yet. Everyone does this, which means the brochure for Chicago and the brochure for Cape Town read identically once you remove the photographs.

The version that works sells the specific thing the student will be able to do afterwards, and treats them as someone with an existing interest rather than someone to be enthused at.

Case 07 · Cinemark · Entertainment
Digital Marketing · Jan 2016 — Jan 2019
07

You get a poster, a trailer and one weekend.

Cinema marketing is the most honest job in the industry. Friday tells you whether you were right.

The thinking

The studio sells the film. The exhibitor sells the decision to leave the house on a specific evening. Confusing them is why most cinema copy is a synopsis with an emoji on the end.

Nobody has ever chosen a Friday night because a film was described as unforgettable.

The rule I wrote to

Never describe a film with a word the film itself would not use. Gripping, unforgettable and edge-of-your-seat are what you write when you have not decided what you think.

Release campaign · Social & site
GET OUT

A weekend at the girlfriend’s parents’ house. That is the entire premise, and it is the last sentence of this description that will feel safe.

Jordan Peele’s first film as a director changed what a horror film is allowed to be about. It is funny for about eleven minutes. Then it is not.

Two customers, one airline, and nothing in common.

The situation

Social and CRM copy for a major international airline, including the segmentation work behind loyalty communication and the executive aviation side of the business — consumer and corporate, under one brand.

The thinking

Loyalty communication is the hardest brief in this business, because the reader has already bought. What is left is whether the message is worth the interruption.

The best deliverability strategy is having something to say.
